# SEATBELT — PayPal-grounded role research (2026-11-08)

**Headline:** PayPal has NO human role doing Seatbelt's pre-payment job (intercept F&F demand → convert to G&S invoice before money moves). At PayPal that prevention is product+policy (payment-type choice at send + User Agreement ban). The real human role Seatbelt mirrors is POST-transaction claims work. Confidence 0.9.

## Real title
- **Payments & Disputes Specialist** (role line in a live PayPal posting; header title "Customer Solutions Teammate (Payments)", dept: Customer Services within Account Protection & Financial Services). Conf 0.9.
- Also: "Customer Service Teammate in Disputes and Claims" (PayPal posting via WayUp). Conf 0.85.
- PayPal publishes NO public title taxonomy/org chart for this function (stated plainly by researcher).

## What the real role does (from PayPal's own postings)
Frontline contact; supports customers filing claims for Item Not Received / Significantly Not As Described + transaction/payment issues; investigates; educates customers on PayPal products/protections (Resolution Center, Purchase/Seller Protection, Claims, Chargebacks, Policy); champions the customer's issue to complete resolution; escalates; confidentiality/security compliance. The PROGRAM decides — specialist supports/guides/investigates/educates; PayPal decides eligibility in its sole discretion. Specialist cannot guarantee a refund, override exclusions, or reopen a closed dispute.

## Hard PayPal facts Seatbelt must speak
- F&F/personal payments are CATEGORICALLY ineligible for Buyer Protection (terms' ineligible list). Conf 0.98.
- NO way to recategorize a payment after it's sent — Seatbelt can only work BEFORE the send. Conf 0.9.
- Coverage = ONLY Item Not Received + Significantly Not As Described. Changed-mind etc. expressly not SNAD. Conf 0.98.
- Windows: INR dispute within 180 days of payment; SNAD within 30 days of delivery or 180 days of payment (whichever sooner); escalate dispute→claim within 20 days or it auto-closes and cannot be reopened. Conf 0.98.
- Ineligible list incl.: real estate, vehicles (except portable light), businesses, industrial machinery, cash equivalents, gold, financial products, NFTs, gambling, donations/crowdfunding, gov/bill-pay, custom-made SNAD, resale-intended, AUP-prohibited, Payouts/Mass Pay, payments not from your PayPal account.
- No return-shipping reimbursement; no double recovery (chargeback closes PayPal claim and vice versa); PayPal's decision final (appeal only with new info).
- Seller ASKING a buyer to pay F&F for goods/services is a User Agreement violation; sanction: PayPal may remove the seller's ability to accept F&F payments. Conf 0.95.
- Invoicing (HELP319): seller issues the invoice (Pay & Get Paid → Create an Invoice); buyer pays it. So Seatbelt's conversion = producing the invoice for the seller to issue / the invoice itself.
- Cost of conversion (Merchant Fees schedule, upd. Sep 1 2026): invoicing via PayPal Checkout/Venmo/Guest 3.49% + $0.49; standard card/wallets 2.99% + fixed; Pay Later 4.99% + fixed; Pay by Bank 1% capped $10; international +1.50%. Seller pays; buyer pays no fee to send G&S.

## Grounded job description
Seatbelt = Payments & Disputes Specialist (pre-payment): identifies an F&F demand as a purchase routed onto a payment type with zero Purchase Protection (and a request sellers are prohibited from making), converts the deal to a real G&S invoice, discloses true cost + true coverage (INR/SNAD only, windows above, exclusion list), states plainly that money already sent as F&F cannot be protected, and never promises a refund.

Sources: PayPal Buyer Protection terms (upd. 26 Jan 2026), US User Agreement, Help HELP277/HELP319/HELP246/HELP238, Merchant Fees schedule PDF, PayPal postings via Indeed/WayUp (URLs in researcher report, 2026-11-08).
